# Seam – Design Document

As of 2026-11-08.

## Vision

Seam is a roguelike, twin-stick dungeon-crawling shooter where the rooms obey a consistent non-Euclidean geometry that the player has to learn. Action is the main thing; the geometry puzzle is the seasoning.

- **Action first.** Fast twin-stick combat is the core loop.
- **Normal until it isn't.** Early floors feel ordinary; the player gradually works out what is different.
- **Strictly consistent logic.** The rules of the geometry never change between runs; only the parameters do (curvature, tiling, wrapping). Inconsistency is a bug, not a feature.
- **Every run is unique.** Unique layout, and every room looks and feels distinct.
- **Learnable.** The geometry is the puzzle, so the game gives a reason to learn it (the Hunter).

## World and geometry

- **Discrete rooms**, each a regular polygon, each unique in look and feel.
- **Unique layout every run;** not every wall has a door.
- **Uniform curvature:** each floor lives on a flat, hyperbolic or spherical surface, built from regular or mixed tilings (e.g. 4.6.12).
- **Wrapped worlds** (torus, Klein bottle) are the flat plane glued by translations and glide reflections.
- **Real geometry, not teleport logic.** Rooms are placed by isometries; walking once round a corner turns you by that corner's angle deficit or excess.
- **Mirrored seams flip the view, never the controls.** Controls are always in screen space. Enemies do not rotate with the room.
- **Inner walls** split some rooms in two: *solid* walls are permanent, *gated* walls open when your side is cleared. Every section must stay reachable from the start.
- **Fixed rules, varying parameters:** the same logic across runs, different numbers each run.

## Gameplay

- **Twin-stick combat:** move and aim independently, in screen space.
- **The Hunter** forces you to learn the map. It always takes the true shortest route (section by section), warns at the door it will use, can be shot, and returns. Gated walls do not stop it; solid walls do.
- **Enemies** spawn telegraphed, never too close to you, in numbers scaled to room area. Each section of a split room spawns its own enemies on first entry.
- **Exit pad** must be reachable, and only counts from the section it sits in.
- **Health pickups** drop from enemies; the drop rate rises slowly with depth.
- **Stairs** lead to the next floor (+1 health). Death starts a new run at floor 1.
- **Bugs that were fixed and must stay fixed:** enemies spawning too close, too many enemies for the room size, exit pad in unreachable spots.

## Difficulty and progression

Difficulty rises smoothly with the floor from one function (`seamjax/difficulty.py`). Floor 1 is small and flat; by floor 6 the full mix of worlds, full-size dungeons and walls are in play. Enemy count, speed and Hunter speed keep rising until floor 24, then stay put (the run itself is endless).

| Floor | World mix | Dungeon size (share of full) | Rooms with inner walls | Ranged enemies | Hunter sets off after |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | flat only | 40% | 0% | 12% | 22 s |
| 2 | 50% flat, 50% wrapped | 52% | 6% | 15% | 20 s |
| 3 | 25% flat, 20% hyperbolic, 20% spherical, 35% wrapped | 64% | 12% | 18% | 17 s |
| 5 | 42% hyperbolic, 23% spherical, 35% wrapped | 88% | 24% | 24% | 11 s |
| 6+ | same as 5 | 100% | 30% (from floor 6) | rises to 45% | 10 s (floor 6+) |

Health drops rise slowly with depth (14% to 24%) to offset the pressure.

## Technical and RL requirements

- **Clean code, no special-casing.** One geometry core; behaviours follow from it.
- **JAX simulation** that can run thousands of games at once on a GPU (about 61k steps/s on a laptop CPU; about 900k on a GTX 1660 SUPER with 8192 games).
- **Everything generated on the device:** the tiling tables (built once by a jitted program, ~4 s) and every dungeon (layout, loops, room shapes, walls, gating, spawn points, exit).
- **Fidelity to the prototype:** the JAX simulation matches the original JS game frame for frame (288,000 frames, no mismatches).
- **Backends:** CPU, NVIDIA CUDA, AMD ROCm and Apple Metal through uv extras (`uv sync --extra cuda|cuda12|rocm|metal`), chosen automatically at start-up (`seamjax/backend.py`; `--device` / `SEAM_BACKEND` to force one). Only the CPU is tested by me; the GPU backends are untested.
- **Playable:** `python -m seamjax.play` (WASD + mouse, or gamepad). The room is drawn on the GPU with OpenGL (floor shaders per world type, glow, particles, bloom, shake); `--software` falls back to pygame drawing. Sound is generated by JAX too (sweeps, FM bells, noise, per-world drones, a Hunter heartbeat); `--mute` turns it off.
- **Gymnasium interface:** single env `Seam-v0` and a vectorised `SeamVectorEnv`; vector or pixel observations; action = move xy + aim xy in screen space.
- **Run-based episodes:** stairs continue the run; death ends the episode.
- **Reward:** kill +0.1, hit −0.2, new room +0.05, health +0.05, gate opened +0.05, stairs +5, death −5.
- **Structure:** `seamjax/` (game), `tests/` (all tests, `run_all.py`), `tools/` (benchmark, samples); no generated or exported data in the distribution.

## Status, not yet built, open questions

**Built:** everything above, in a JS prototype (browser) and in JAX (playable, Gymnasium, GPU-ready).

**Not yet built (ideas listed, no decision):**

- Collectibles (art, creatures), character classes, leveling and ability unlocks, a skill tree, a home base, co-op and competitive multiplayer.

**Open questions:**

- Does an RL agent learn the geometry? (A recurrent PPO training loop is the obvious next step.)
- GPU speed: measured on a GTX 1660 SUPER (CUDA): about 900k steps/s with 8192 games. The step's remaining big costs are the per-enemy loops that place bullets and pickups, and the Hunter tick.
- Ideas offered but not chosen: a déjà-vu ping when you re-enter a seen room, door tinting.
